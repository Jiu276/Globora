Explore MC2 Saint Barth's Pre-Fall Mid-Season Jackets & Overshirts

I went to the coast with one carry-on and the wrong coat. What saved the trip wasn't a trend list—it was learning what mid-season layering actually feels like.

Luca Moretti Style Editor · Globora
September 8, 2026
9 min read
1.4k views
Share:

The Ligurian coast in early September—still summer in the photos, already autumn once the sun drops.

Last September I spent a week between Monterosso and Portofino with a suitcase that was, frankly, optimistic. I packed like it was still August. The coast had other plans—sun at noon, wind that cut through a cotton shirt by nine. That awkward mid-season weather is when most of us dress badly. This is what I actually wore, and why MC2 Saint Barth—a label I only knew for swim—suddenly made sense for jackets, overshirts, and everything in between.

The week I packed wrong

I brought a navy blazer because blazers feel adult. I wore it once, to dinner, and spent the whole walk back clutching the lapels like that would generate heat. During the day I rotated between a linen shirt (too cold) and a fleece I stole from a friend's boat (too sloppy for the trattoria we kept returning to). Not a crisis—just the small, daily annoyance of misreading pre-fall weather.

On day three my partner said, quietly, "You look cold in every photo." She wasn't wrong.

A brand I'd only filed under "swim"

I'd seen MC2 Saint Barth on the beach for years—the loud prints, the easy polos, the kind of confidence that doesn't need an occasion. I'd never thought of them for September. Then a shop in Santa Margherita had their pre-fall rail out early, and the jackets and overshirts didn't look like afterthoughts. They looked like someone had actually lived through a week like mine and designed for it.

I didn't buy anything that afternoon. I went back the next morning before the ferry to look at mid-season jackets one more time, which is either restraint or procrastination, depending on your relationship with shopping.

The jacket that almost stayed in the bag

I tried on a lightweight technical shell—the Parson line, if you care about names—and rolled my eyes a little. Another "performance" jacket. Then I walked outside and the Ligurian wind did its thing. The fabric blocked it without that crinkly raincoat sound. I bought it because I was tired of being cold, not because I love outdoor gear.

If you're browsing the current mid-season jackets online, the range is wider than I expected: wool-blend shirt-jackets for dry evenings, unlined bombers if you want something sharper, padded gilets when sleeves feel like too much commitment. I'm still not a "fifty options" person—but I get why they exist. Different coasts, different moods.

Honest note: mine ran slightly roomy. Fine for a sweater underneath. If you want a cleaner line, size down—and compare chest measurements on it.mc2saintbarth.com before you order.

The overshirt I wore four days straight

The Chalet tartan—open at lunch, buttoned when the terrace heaters weren't enough.

The surprise was a Chalet overshirt in a muted tartan—not my usual print, but it didn't scream on camera, which mattered more than I'd admit. Chest pockets that actually hold a phone. Weight between shirt and jacket. I wore it open over a white tee during the day, closed it at night, once slept in it on the train because I was too tired to dig in my bag.

There's an embroidered Esteban in navy if you want something quieter with a crest detail, and wool-blend Landeck styles if your September is closer to mountain air than sea air. I'm not going to pretend I tried them all. The overshirts section on the Italian site is the one I'd scroll with a coffee, not a checklist.

Price stung a little—think €250–€300—and I hesitated at the register. I've worn it maybe thirty times since. The math stopped bothering me around wear twelve.

What I'd pack again

If I did the same trip tomorrow, one carry-on, I'd take: the technical shell for wind and sudden rain, one patterned overshirt for dinners and photos, a thin merino underneath, and I'd leave the blazer at home. That's it. My partner would still steal the gilet I didn't buy but should have.

The trattoria we kept going back to—where I finally stopped thinking about whether I was cold.

Women I traveled with borrowed the men's cuts—belted, sleeves rolled—and looked better than I did. The brand's women's line has its own outerwear, but don't ignore the men's rail if you like structure.

If you're shopping pre-fall right now

September is when brands drop the mid-season edit and ads start following you around—fair. I'm writing this the same week the new pieces are live because that's when the question is real, not theoretical. If you want to see what I was squinting at in that shop, the MC2 Saint Barth Italian store is where I'd start; EU duties are sorted on that site, and shipping thresholds are spelled out clearly, which saved me a boring email to customer service.

I don't think you need a wardrobe full of logo prints. One good layer that handles wind, one overshirt you'll reach for without thinking—that's the useful part. The rest is vacation fantasy, and that's fine too.
